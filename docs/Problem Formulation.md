# Problem Statement & Scope
Rapid KL bus commuters in Greater KL. The new PULSE app that launched in May 2025 approximately has 240,000 downloads, 96,000 active users and 34,426 weekly users 
of real-time bus tracking (Marlia Z., 2026). PULSE shows bus departure times from the terminal but not arrival times at intermediate stops and does not clearly separate scheduled times from real-time estimates. Commuters say the times are "not always accurate" (Gregory H. W. S. et al., 2025). Commuters wait at stops without knowing when the bus will come ,which wastes time and lowers trust in public transport. Some may switch to e-hailing.


Main users

- Daily bus commuters which is students and workers in the Klang Valley
- Occasional riders and tourists unfamiliar with routes

Stakeholders

- Prasarana or Rapid Bus Sdn Bhd (operator and app owner)
- Bus operations controllers (monitor service on the road)
- Jabatan Digital Negara or data.gov.my (publishes the GTFS open data)
- Agensi Pengangkutan Awam Darat (APAD) and Ministry of Transport (regulators)

In scope

- Rapid KL stage buses in the Klang Valley
- Predicting bus arrival time at each stop on a route
- Showing predicted vs scheduled time, and alerts for saved stops
- Using historical and live GTFS vehicle-position data

Out of scope

- LRT, MRT and Monorail trains
- Ticketing, My50 pass, payments
- Full journey planning (A to B routing)
- Bus crowding/passenger counts (the data has no boarding info)
- Installing new GPS hardware on buses
- Rapid Penang and Rapid Kuantan
